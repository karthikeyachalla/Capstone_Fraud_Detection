1. "Why only SMOTE when we have advanced techniques for class imbalance?"

  
Why the professor is right: SMOTE was invented in 2002. It creates synthetic points by drawing a straight line between two nearest minority points. In complex financial fraud, this causes severe problems: it synthesizes fake transactions in empty feature space, generates unrealistic combinations, and completely ignores the time component.

  
The Modern Alternatives in 2026:
  
  

  
Focal Loss / Class-Balanced Loss: Instead of generating fake data, the loss function dynamically penalizes the model more for misclassifying rare fraud samples, forcing it to learn hard examples without synthetic distortion.

  
Cost-Sensitive Threshold Optimization: In banking, the financial cost of a False Negative (losing ₹50,000 to fraud) is 100x higher than a False Positive (sending a 2FA OTP for ₹1.50). You adjust the classification threshold based on monetary cost curves rather than raw 50/50 balance.

  
Generative Tabular Synthesis (CTGAN / TabDDPM): Conditional GANs or Diffusion models designed specifically for tabular constraints (preserving discrete categorical columns and continuous distributions).

Part 3: Answering Your Professor's Tough Questions
Here are the modern, 2026-level answers to the exact questions your professor asked:
1. "Why only SMOTE when we have advanced techniques for class imbalance?"
Why the professor is right: SMOTE was invented in 2002. It creates synthetic points by drawing a straight line between two nearest minority points. In complex financial fraud, this causes severe problems: it synthesizes fake transactions in empty feature space, generates unrealistic combinations, and completely ignores the time component.
The Modern Alternatives in 2026:Focal Loss / Class-Balanced Loss: Instead of generating fake data, the loss function dynamically penalizes the model more for misclassifying rare fraud samples, forcing it to learn hard examples without synthetic distortion.
Cost-Sensitive Threshold Optimization: In banking, the financial cost of a False Negative (losing ₹50,000 to fraud) is 100x higher than a False Positive (sending a 2FA OTP for ₹1.50). You adjust the classification threshold based on monetary cost curves rather than raw 50/50 balance.
Generative Tabular Synthesis (CTGAN / TabDDPM): Conditional GANs or Diffusion models designed specifically for tabular constraints (preserving discrete categorical columns and continuous distributions).
2. "Explain about Drift (What is it really?)"
Fraud detection is one of the few ML domains where the data actively fights against you. There are two distinct types of drift:
Covariate Shift / Data Drift (P(X)P(X) changes):The distribution of incoming inputs changes, but the fraud behavior stays the same.Example: During Diwali or Big Billion Day sales, the average transaction amount and frequency spike dramatically across all users legitimately. If your model isn't drift-aware, it flags thousands of legitimate shoppers as fraudsters.
Concept Drift (P(Y∣X)P(Y∣X) changes):The underlying relationship between features and fraud changes because fraudsters change their tactics.Example: Once banks started blocking large nighttime transfers, fraudsters shifted to "smurfing" (sending 20 micro-transfers of ₹1,999 during business hours). The old rules no longer catch the new behavior.
How to Detect It: We use statistical distance metrics over streaming time windows:Kolmogorov-Smirnov (KS) Test: Compares feature probability distributions between baseline training data and the last 24 hours of streaming data.
Population Stability Index (PSI): Quantifies shift; PSI>0.2PSI>0.2 triggers an automated alert indicating the model must be retrained.
3. "Is GAT really working? How do ML and GNN work at the same time?"
The Honest Truth: In real production systems (like PayPal, Visa, or Alibaba), you never run a full Graph Attention Network inside a 5ms payment gateway loop. Graph propagation over millions of nodes is computationally too heavy to finish in 5ms.
How Real Banks Actually Combine ML + GNN:Near-Line / Batch (The GNN): Runs every hour or day in the background. It analyzes the graph of accounts, device IDs, and IP addresses to find clusters and computes a Graph Risk Embedding or Centrality Score for each customer/device.
Real-Time (The LightGBM): When a transaction arrives at the API, the gateway looks up that pre-computed graph score from a fast cache (Redis) and feeds it as a single feature into LightGBM in <3ms!
The Academic Defense: This is called a Two-Tier Heterogeneous Architecture (Offline Graph Representation Learning + Online Gradient Boosted Scoring).
4. "Is Autoencoder just feature extraction? Why not modern models when AI is booming?"
The Professor's Point: An autoencoder compresses features into a bottleneck layer and reconstructs them. If you just take the bottleneck vector, it's just non-linear PCA (feature extraction).
How an Autoencoder IS used for Fraud (Semi-Supervised Anomaly Detection):You train the Autoencoder only on legitimate transactions. It learns the mathematical "grammar" of normal human spending. When an unseen, zero-day fraud pattern appears, the Autoencoder cannot reconstruct it—the reconstruction error (MSE) spikes. That error itself is the anomaly score.

2. "Explain about Drift (What is it really?)"

Covariate Shift / Data Drift (P(X)P(X) changes):The distribution of incoming inputs changes, but the fraud behavior stays the same.Example: During Diwali or Big Billion Day sales, the average transaction amount and frequency spike dramatically across all users legitimately. If your model isn't drift-aware, it flags thousands of legitimate shoppers as fraudsters.
Concept Drift (P(Y∣X)P(Y∣X) changes):The underlying relationship between features and fraud changes because fraudsters change their tactics.Example: Once banks started blocking large nighttime transfers, fraudsters shifted to "smurfing" (sending 20 micro-transfers of ₹1,999 during business hours).
